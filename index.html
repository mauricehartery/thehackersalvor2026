Laguna Golf Course

One of five courses in the Vilamoura golf cluster, Laguna sits on low-lying coastal land — flatter and more open than its neighbours, with a distinct links feel despite being inland. There's next to no rough to punish a stray tee shot, but water comes into play on eleven of the eighteen holes, so accuracy over distance is the name of the game. The back nine turns for home along the toughest stretch of the course, especially with any wind off the coast. Par 72.

Palmares Golf Course

A 27-hole Robert Trent Jones Jr. design split into three nine-hole loops — Alvor, Lagos and Praia — each with its own character. Alvor is hilly parkland through pine corridors with views to the Monchique hills; Lagos mixes parkland and lakes; Praia is the headline act, true links golf through the dunes with the Atlantic in view on almost every hole. The clubhouse, designed by Pritzker Prize winners RCR Architects, looks out over the Alvor Estuary and Meia Praia beach. Closest course to base by some distance.

Faldo Course, Amendoeira

Sir Nick Faldo's only design in the Algarve, set inland near Silves amid olive groves and rocky outcrops with a distinctly desert-golf look — crushed limestone bunkers standing in for the sandy tracts of Faldo's English home courses. It's a strategic, position-first layout that punishes anyone chasing distance over placement, with the more dramatic, undulating holes saved for the back nine. Fitting territory for the last round of the trip. Par 72.

The Competition

A three-day pairs event, one format per round, running across all three courses.

Round I · Laguna · Fri 9 Oct

Chapman

Both players in a team tee off, then swap balls for the second shot — you play your partner's drive, they play yours. From there, the team picks the better of the two second shots and plays alternate shot the rest of the way to the hole. Rewards a good drive and a smart choice almost as much as the golf itself.

Round II · Palmares · Sat 10 Oct

Fourball

Better ball. Everyone plays their own ball for all 18 holes, and the team score on each hole is whichever partner scored lower. Straightforward, forgiving of a bad hole, and the format most likely to produce a genuinely good scorecard.

Round III · Faldo · Sun 11 Oct

Champagne Scramble

Everyone in the team tees off, the best shot is picked, and both players then play their next shot from that spot — repeating all the way to the hole. Fast, sociable, and the format that keeps both players in every hole regardless of who's playing well. A fitting, relaxed way to close out the trip.

Food

Recommendations to add to the list — more to come as dinners get locked in.

Cult n' Art Café

A small, warm café-slash-concept-store in Alvor's old town, run by a couple who take real pride in fresh, homemade food — think shakshuka, granola bowls, toasts, fresh juices, and proper coffee. Great shout for a breakfast or brunch morning before golf. Open Tuesday to Saturday, 9:00–15:00 — closed Mondays and Sundays.

R. do Castelo 18, Alvor

Wild's Sandwich Boutique

Alvor's top-rated sandwich spot — homemade bread, breakfast baps, Irish sausages and black pudding, cinnamon rolls, and proper coffee, all run by a genuinely friendly team who remember faces. Good for a takeaway sandwich on the way to golf or a sit-down brunch. Open daily except Mondays, 9:00–4:00pm (Thursdays until 1:00pm).

R. Q.ta da Praia, Alvor
Open in Maps → View Menu →

Albar

The go-to for late-night food in Alvor — piri-piri chicken, kebabs, burgers, fish and chips, all at reasonable prices, with outdoor seating right in the town centre. Does takeaway too, and stays open until 1am every night — the obvious call after pints on Friday.

R. Padre David Neto, Alvor
Open in Maps → View Menu →

Churrasqueira Mercado de Alvor

A proper Portuguese churrasqueira, all about piri-piri chicken and grilled fish, simple menu, high turnover, packed with locals. Fast enough for a quick sit-down without eating into the night — a good shout before heading out for pints on Friday instead of takeaway.

Largo do Castelo, Alvor
Open in Maps → View Menu →

Ruccula

An Alvor institution overlooking the harbour, family-run for close to 20 years and consistently one of the best-reviewed spots in town. A modern, varied menu — lamb shank, sea bass and fillet steak are the standouts — with a big, well-priced wine list. Booked out most nights, so worth getting there early to catch the sunset over the water. Our pick for the first night of the trip.

R. Poeta João de Deus, Alvor
Open in Maps → View Menu →

Mumbai Darbar

Alvor's top-rated Indian — ranked in the top handful of restaurants in town, with nearly a thousand reviews averaging 4.8/5. The big draw is fully customisable spice levels, so everyone from mild to very hot is covered at the same table. Standouts include the Chicken Lababdar, Butter Chicken, Saag Paneer and the Peshwari naan. Handles large groups well — worth booking ahead for 12 on a Saturday night.

Time Zone

Portugal is on the same time as Ireland this week — no watch-changing needed.

Power

Portugal uses 2-pin Type C/F sockets, different to Ireland's 3-pin — bring an adapter.

Currency

Euro. Cards widely accepted, but a fair few spots in Alvor are cash only — plenty of ATMs around the town, so worth keeping some cash on hand for taxis, small bars, and those places.

Emergency Number

112 works across the EU for police, ambulance or fire.

Tipping

Not obligatory in Portugal — rounding up or 5–10% for good service is normal, not expected.

Group Size

12 golfers travelling — Bourkey joins for the golf, the other 11 fly out together.
